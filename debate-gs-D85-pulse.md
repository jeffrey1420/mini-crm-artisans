# Growth Strategist — Debate D85 Pulse

**Role:** Growth Strategist, Mini-CRM project (devis/factures for French artisans)
**Debate:** D85 — GetApp/Capterra timing
**Pulse:** Challenging unexamined assumption from both D116 and D117

---

## 1. The Assumption Being Challenged

Both D116 (defer to Month 3) and D117 (claim Week 1 for ranking moat) share a foundational premise that neither has examined: **the admin handler discovers software on GetApp/Capterra.**

This assumption appears implicitly in both positions. D116 argues comparison sites are "mid-to-low funnel" tools the admin handler will eventually consult. D117 argues the admin handler searches comparison sites early in her journey, creating competitive displacement risk. Both assume GetApp/Capterra sits on the admin handler's consideration path.

D55 invalidates this premise entirely.

---

## 2. The Argument

D55 explicitly defines the admin handler's role: she is an **operational validator**, not a prospective discoverer. Her journey looks like this:

1. **Marc discovers the product via WhatsApp peer referral** — his mate says "j'utilise ça, c'est génial"
2. **Marc tries the Free tier** — he's motivated, acute-need buyer, signs up same day
3. **Marc's wife encounters the product when he asks for help with setup** — or when she notices him using it while doing administrative work
4. **Her role is operational validation:** "Can this handle our specific client types? Will it produce correct French legally-compliant invoices?" — answered by opening the app and creating a test devis, not by reading a GetApp listing
5. **GetApp is consulted only if Marc is comparing two specific options** — which he won't do if his WhatsApp peer gave a confident, specific recommendation ("j'ai 40 clients dessus, je l'utilise depuis 2 ans")

The result: **GetApp/Capterra ranking position is irrelevant to this buyer journey.** Marc doesn't reach the comparison stage. The peer referral closes the deal before GetApp becomes relevant. The admin handler doesn't independently search for software — she evaluates what Marc has already chosen.

This is not a timing debate. It's a channel-suits-buyer-match debate. The question isn't "Week 1 or Month 3" — it's "does this channel even function for this buyer journey?" For Marc and his admin handler, it doesn't.

The 8-10 hours required to claim, optimize, populate screenshots, seed reviews, and maintain GetApp/Capterra profiles in Week 1 would be better spent on:
- The prescriber outreach playbook (D72 Phase 2 prep) — moving actual humans into the product
- The expert-comptable cold call script — getting 3 accountants to see the product in Week 1
- Guerrilla usability testing with real artisans at Point P on Saturday morning

GetApp optimization moves nothing. Prescriber outreach moves humans.

---

## 3. The Proposed Resolution

**GetApp/Capterra is not a Week 1 priority — it is not a priority at all for this GTM model.**

If the admin handler discovers via peer referral (Marc) and validates via product usage, comparison sites serve zero functions in the actual buyer journey. The D85 GetApp/Capterra action item should be removed from the TODO and revisited only if:
- Evidence emerges that admin handlers independently search comparison sites before peer referral
- SEO data shows "devis facture artisan" queries driving meaningful traffic to GetApp/Capterra
- Month 3 metrics show zero peer referral attribution (proving the WhatsApp hypothesis was wrong)

Until then: invest in moving humans, not optimizing listings.
