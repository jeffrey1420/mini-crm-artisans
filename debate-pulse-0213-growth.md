# Position Paper: GS-D147 — Annual Billing Default Cannibalizes Free-to-Paid Conversions

**Author:** Growth Strategist (subagent)
**Date:** 2026-03-31
**Pulse:** 01:59, debate GS-D147

---

## 1. Default at Checkout: Monthly €29

Monthly €29 must be the primary, prominently displayed option at checkout. Annual €240/year is a secondary, opt-in savings display — not a default.

**The rationale:** At the moment of first payment, Marc has sent exactly one devis. He has not experienced relances, the document archive, the financial snapshot, or any €29-tier differentiator. He has validated the Free tier. He has not validated the premium tier. Presenting €240 upfront anchors the product as "expensive" before value is established. Showing €29 as the clean, confident, primary choice removes cognitive friction at the exact moment conversion is most fragile.

Annual €240 as DEFAULT was argued at 01:15 (GS-D145) using the 35-45% uptake figure. That figure is inapplicable here. It describes coerced uptake under default bias — not genuine conversion. Artisans who select annual because it is pre-selected are not demonstrating product-market fit. They are avoiding a UI pattern. When the first renewal hits and cash flow is tight, that coerced conversion becomes involuntary churn.

**Resolution:** Monthly €29 is the confirmed default. Annual €240 appears below as a positive savings choice — "Épargnez €108/an" — not as the primary frame.

---

## 2. Pricing Page Architecture

**Three-zone layout:**

**Zone A (primary):** €29/mois. No framing as fallback. Copy: *"Simple, flexible, sans engagement."* Single CTA button. This is the product's confident voice.

**Zone B (secondary, below fold):** €240/an. Copy: *"Soit €20/mois — vous épargnez €108 par an."* Annual framed as savings, not commitment. A second CTA: *"Payer annuellement."*

**Zone C (conversion moment only):** After 30+ days of active usage (5+ devis sent OR 3+ archived documents), present annual as a contextual upgrade prompt — not at initial checkout. At this point Marc has experienced the product. He has sent multiple devis. The annual ask lands on demonstrated value, not faith.

**What this architecture rejects:**
- Annual-first presentation ("€240 today, or €29/month")
- "Payez quand vous êtes chargé" — seasonal framing conflates billing cycle with product value
- Sticker-shock anchoring at the first payment moment

---

## 3. Challenged Assumption: "35-45% Annual Uptake Is Achievable"

**The challenged assumption:** D138 (01:15) cited 35-45% annual uptake under default-annual framing. This figure originates from SaaS benchmarks where annual billing is presented to users with months of product experience — Netflix, Spotify, Adobe. In those contexts, default annual converts because the user already knows the product's value. They are prepaying for something they trust.

**Why this is wrong for Mini-CRM at launch:**
- Marc has zero completed payment cycles at first conversion
- One devis sent ≠ product trust
- French artisans have project-based income — €240 upfront in a slow month is a genuine barrier regardless of annual savings arithmetic
- Coerced annual conversions generate involuntary churn at renewal, not retained revenue

**The alternative benchmark:** Opt-in annual uptake at 10-15% is the realistic target for month-1 conversions — users who genuinely prefer annual billing because they've decided the product is worth prepaying. This is healthier signal. It measures product conviction, not UI default bias.

**KPI:** 40%+ of monthly users who reach engagement triggers (5+ devis sent) upgrade to annual within 60 days. This measures product-market fit, not conversion pressure.
