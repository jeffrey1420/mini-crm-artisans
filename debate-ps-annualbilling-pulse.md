# DEBATE: D138 — Annual Billing at Launch
**Position: PS-D152 — ELIMINATE annual billing entirely. Monthly €29 only. No exceptions.**
**Author: Product Strategist (Pulse)**
**Date: 2026-03-31**

---

## The Ask

Ship with **one SKU: €29/month**. That is it. No annual. No "save €96." No opt-in tier below the monthly option. Nothing.

Annual billing is a v2 conversation. We do not have product-market fit. We are not ready to have that conversation.

---

## My Five Arguments (Brief)

1. **Annual masks seasonality churn.** A customer who prepaid through a slow Q1 never tells you "this tool didn't pull its weight." They just... cancel at renewal. Louis gets silence instead of signal.

2. **Annual is a second SKU before PMF.** Copy, onboarding flows, churn logic, accounting entries, LTV models — all doubled before we know if the first SKU works.

3. **Annual framing poisons monthly perception.** Even as opt-in, showing €20/month alongside €29/month makes €29 feel expensive. You're not comparing value — you're training buyers to feel ripped off.

4. **€240 upfront IS expensive, full stop.** For a solo artisan, €240 is real money. "It's only €20/month equivalent" is a SaaS sleight of hand. The cash leaves their account today.

5. **Monthly is the honest tier.** It measures whether the product delivers value every single month. If it can't hold a month, it deserves to churn.

---

## Responding to GS-D147

### GS says: "Annual masks seasonality — but monthly ALSO hides it. It just shows as churn. So why is monthly better?"

Monthly churn **is** the signal. That's the point.

When a customer cancels in February, Louis sees: "February — didn't renew." That's actionable. He can ask why. He can improve. He can notice a pattern across his user base.

When a customer prepaid annual and cancels in February? Louis sees... nothing. They're locked in. The churn signal doesn't fire until March, and by then you've lost an entire quarter of learning.

GS is arguing that monthly churn and annual churn are equivalent signal loss. They're not. Annual **delays** the signal. Monthly **delivers** it on time. Delay is not the same as absence — but delay at a startup is nearly as fatal. You don't have time to wait.

Monthly churn tells you **when** the product failed. Annual tells you it failed — eventually — when it's too late to do anything about it.

---

### GS says: "€240 is expensive upfront — but it's the same cash outflow as monthly, just differently timed. So what's the difference?"

The difference is **psychology and commitment level at the moment of decision.**

When Louis is evaluating a new tool he's never used, asking him to commit €240 upfront is asking him to bet on a relationship that hasn't started. That's a trust load the product hasn't earned.

€29/month says: "Try this. If it works, stay. If it doesn't, leave." That's the correct framing for a v1 product selling to skeptical artisans.

The cash timing argument is technically true but strategically naive. Yes, €240 over 12 months ≈ €20/month equivalent. But:

- **Annuities require trust the product hasn't built yet.** Monthly requires only that the first month delivers value.
- **€240 anchors "big purchase" psychology.** €29/month anchors "small monthly operating cost" psychology. These are completely different mental accounts for a small business owner.
- **Cognitive load matters at conversion.** Every additional pricing option increases decision friction. We want Louis to say "that's a no-brainer €29/month" — not "hmm, €240 or €29?"

GS's argument treats pricing as pure math. It's not. Pricing is psychology. Same numbers, different framing, different conversion rates.

---

## What GS Gets Right (And Why It Doesn't Change My Position)

GS is correct that:
- Annual-first anchoring destroys first conversion (their Argument 1)
- Monthly handles seasonality naturally (their Argument 3)
- Annual should be an upsell to retained users (their Argument 4)

I agree with all three of those points.

But notice what GS is describing: **a world where monthly is primary and annual is introduced later, as a retention play.** That is functionally identical to what I'm proposing — except GS still wants annual in the product at launch.

The question isn't "annual or no annual at all, ever." The question is "annual or no annual at launch." I say: no annual at launch.

We can add annual billing six months from now when we have retained users to upsell it to. Right now, we have zero retained users and one job: get the first cohort through their first month and into their second.

---

## Summary

| | PS-D152 (Me) | GS-D147 |
|---|---|---|
| Launch SKU | Monthly only | Monthly + annual opt-in |
| Annual timing | v2, post-PMF | v1, opt-in below monthly |
| Signal clarity | Churn fires monthly | Churn delayed up to 12 months |
| Decision friction | Single choice | Dual choice |
| Trust required | First month earns it | Eight months prepaid upfront |

GS's position is reasonable in a world where we've already validated the product. We haven't. Monthly only at launch. Annual later. Done.

---

**Recommendation: DECISION D138 — ELIMINATE annual billing at launch. Monthly €29 ONLY. Revisit annual at 6-month review with actual retention data.**
