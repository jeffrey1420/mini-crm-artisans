# Growth Strategist — Pulse D146

## Challenge: D138 and D139 Are Not Independent Workstreams — Pricing Frame Cross-Pollution Will Poison the Expert-Comptable Conversation

---

### Assumption Being Challenged

The current decisions treat D138 (pricing page) and D139 (expert-comptable outreach in Week 1) as parallel, independent workstreams. D138 sets the primary landing page price at €29/month with "sans engagement" and seasonal framing. D139 schedules expert-comptable problem-discovery calls for Week 1. The implicit assumption: these are separate deliverables that don't contaminate each other. **This assumption is wrong.**

---

### The Specific Argument: Pricing Frame Cross-Pollution

The €29/month "sans engagement" framing solves a consumer-conversion problem. It does not solve — and actively harms — an expert-comptable credibility problem.

Expert-comptables are not artisanal end users. They think in annual contracts, TVA-deductible line items, and client cash-flow management. When Louis describes his product in a Week 1 expert-comptable conversation, the first substantive question will be "ça coûte combien?" If Louis says "€29/month, sans engagement," the response he will get — from someone who manages dozens of client businesses — is something like: *"Les artisans que je connais ne paieraient pas ça par mois."* Or worse: *"Sans engagement, donc sans engagement pour vous aussi?"*

This is not a product validation failure. The product may be entirely valid. This is a **pricing frame mismatch**. The "sans engagement" monthly anchor that converts an artisan scrolling a landing page reads as "no skin in the game" to a professional advisor. It signals low commitment, not low risk. It does not create urgency; it signals that the vendor doesn't believe enough in the product to ask for commitment.

The problem is structural: D138's pricing frame was designed for a consumer landing page. It will be imported into the expert-comptable conversation by default — because Louis will use the same numbers, the same language, the same framing. Unless explicitly corrected, the landing page pricing logic bleeds directly into the relationship-building outreach.

---

### Recommended Resolution

D138 and D139 need a **dual framing protocol** before either is finalized:

1. **Landing page (D138):** Keep €29/month primary, "sans engagement," "Payez quand vous êtes chargé." This frame is correct for artisanal end-user conversion.

2. **Expert-comptable outreach (D139):** Use a different pricing anchor entirely. Two options:
   - *Daily anchor for artisans:* "L'outil sera à €29/mois — moins d'un euro par jour ouvré." Transforms a recurring monthly fee into a daily personal-expense comparison the artisan understands.
   - *Annual/competitive anchor for the expert-comptable:* "C'est moins que ce que vous coûte Sage en licences annuelles par client." Anchors the conversation in a world the expert-comptable already operates in.

These are not the same pitch. They cannot be the same page or the same talking points. Louis needs to walk into Week 1 expert-comptable meetings with a separate, purpose-built pricing narrative — not a copy-paste of the landing page.

---

### Why Louis Should Care

If Louis walks into his first expert-comptable conversations with D138's pricing frame, he risks losing credibility with the single channel most likely to recommend his product to multiple clients at once. A single expert-comptable endorsement can mean 10–20 client referrals. A single dismissed conversation can mean zero.

The €29/month "sans engagement" landing page is not wrong. It's just the wrong tool for expert-comptable outreach. These are different audiences with different mental models. Treating them as the same workstream is how a well-designed conversion page quietly kills a promising B2B channel.

Louis should resolve D138 and D139 together, not sequentially.
