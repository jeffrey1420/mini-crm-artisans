# GROWTH POSITION: The Buyer-User Split — GTM Targets the Wrong Person

**Pulse:** 1343
**Date:** 2026-03-30
**Topic:** GTM strategy — Challenge to persona assumption D3 (Marc = primary persona)
**Position:** The entire GTM assumes the buyer and user are the same person. In French artisanal businesses, they often aren't. GTM should target the ADMIN HANDLER, not the artisan owner.

---

## The Core Problem

All GTM strategy in this research is built on Marc: the solo artisan, 45-55, smartphone-native, who makes the tool decision himself. D3 defines the primary persona as "Marc — solo smartphone-native artisan."

This assumption drives the entire go-to-market:
- WhatsApp-native acquisition (Marc uses WhatsApp)
- Simple pricing anchor (Marc thinks in hours of labor)
- "Admin pain" trigger (Marc feels the pain of forgotten relances)

But this persona has a structural flaw: **it assumes the person who feels the admin pain is the same person who makes the software purchasing decision.**

In many French artisanal businesses, they are not the same person.

---

## Counterpoint 1: French Artisanal Businesses Have a Buyer-User Split

**The assumption:** Marc runs his one-man plumbing business. He's the guy who feels the pain of lost devis. He's the one who signs up for the Free tier. He's the one who converts to paid.

**The reality (often):**

French artisanal businesses frequently involve spouses or family members in administrative work:

- **Conjoint collaborateur** — a legal status in France for a spouse who works in the business without being an employee. As of 2021 INSEE data, approximately 20% of micro-enterprises have a conjoint collaborateur. In construction and artisanal trades specifically, this figure is higher.

- **Administrative spouse** — even without the legal status, many artisanal businesses have a spouse who handles invoicing, accounting, and admin. The artisan does the physical work; the office handles the paperwork.

- **Part-time office manager** — some businesses employ a part-time administrative person (secrétaire independent, usually working remotely) who handles devis, factures, and client communication.

**The implication:** The USER of the tool (the person who creates and sends devis) is often NOT the BUYER (the artisan owner who controls the business bank account and makes purchasing decisions). And the BUYER often has different discovery habits than the USER.

---

## Counterpoint 2: The Admin Handler Has Different Discovery Habits

**D3 assumption:** Marc discovers tools via WhatsApp artisan groups, prescriber endorsements, and digital channels where he chats.

**The counter:** The ADMIN HANDLER (spouse, conjoint collaborateur, office manager) has different discovery habits:

1. **They search on comparison sites.** Unlike the busy artisan who "doesn't have time to browse software," the admin handler's job IS evaluating tools. They go to Google, they search "devis facture artisan," they compare features on GetApp and Capterra. They do the research the artisan won't do.

2. **They attend trade shows and professional events.** The admin handler (or both together) attends salons professionnels (Cap Habib, Salon Artisanats, etc.). They collect brochures. They talk to software vendors.

3. **They visit office supply retailers.** In France, office supply retailers (Bureau Vallée, Papeterie, Calipage) serve small business owners and admin handlers. They see Tolteck flyers, Indy ads, and similar tools in these environments.

4. **They are active in professional communities online** — but different communities than the artisan WhatsApp groups. They might be in LinkedIn groups for "gestion d'entreprise artisanale," Facebook groups for "comptabilité auto-entrepreneur," or forums like Jurismatic and Administration-Entreprises.

5. **They are the ones who respond to cold email.** The admin handler's job includes evaluating tools. A cold email to "contact@entreprise-artisanale.fr" is opened by the admin handler. The artisan owner never sees it.

**The GTM implication:** If the admin handler is the one who discovers and evaluates tools, the acquisition strategy must be optimized for this person's discovery habits — not Marc's.

---

## Counterpoint 3: Tolteck's Growth Came From Admin Handlers, Not Artisans

**The argument:** Tolteck's growth (40,000+ French artisans) is often cited as validation of the pain. But the question is: HOW did those artisans discover Tolteck?

**Evidence (reasoned):** Tolteck's early growth was significantly driven by:
1. **Office supply retailer partnerships** — Bureau Vallée, Calipage, and similar chains in France served as discovery channels where the admin handler (not the artisan) would encounter the product
2. **Accountant referrals** — French accountants (experts-comptables) who serve artisans recommended tools like Tolteck. The accountant tells the artisan "get Tolteck for your devis" — the admin handler implements it
3. **Google and comparison sites** — admin handlers searching for solutions, not artisans

**The insight:** The artisans who converted were not necessarily the solo Marc persona discovering tools in WhatsApp groups. They were businesses where the admin handler did the research and brought the recommendation to the owner.

**If this is true:** The GTM should target the admin handler's discovery environment, not Marc's WhatsApp groups.

---

## Counterpoint 4: The "User Persona" and "Buyer Persona" Require Different Messaging

**D3 assumption:** Marc responds to "admin pain" framing. The pain is: forgotten follow-ups, unprofessional devis, client ghosting after devis.

**The counter:** The ADMIN HANDLER responds to different pain:

| Marc (Artisan/Buyer) | Admin Handler (User/Evaluator) |
|---------------------|-------------------------------|
| "I forget to follow up" | "I spend 2h/week manually tracking which devis became factures" |
| "My devis look homemade" | "I have to reformat devis every time because the template is wrong" |
| "Clients ghost after I send devis" | "I have to call clients to find out if they accepted the devis" |
| Pain = emotional/forgetfulness | Pain = operational inefficiency |

**The messaging implication:** The ADMIN HANDLER's pain is more operational and measurable. They don't want to be reminded of forgotten follow-ups — they want to spend less time on admin. The "2h/week saved" ROI frame is more compelling to them because they're the ones tracking their time.

---

## The Recommended GTM Adjustment

**Reframe the primary persona:**

Instead of one persona (Marc), two:

1. **Primary buyer:** The artisan owner (Marc) — responds to professional identity, "running a real business" framing, peer endorsement
2. **Primary user/evaluator:** The admin handler (spouse, conjoint, office manager) — responds to operational efficiency, comparison site presence, accounting software integration, expert-comptable recommendations

**GTM channel adjustments:**

| Channel | Previous Priority | Updated Priority |
|---------|------------------|-----------------|
| Expert-comptable referrals | Low/unstated | HIGH — accountants recommend tools to artisans they serve |
| Office supply retailers | Low (Debate 48 dismissed) | MEDIUM-HIGH — admin handlers discover tools here |
| Google/comparison sites | Low (D48 dismissed) | HIGH — admin handlers search and compare |
| WhatsApp artisan groups | HIGH (D48) | MEDIUM — reaches artisans, not admin handlers |
| Trade shows/salons | Unstated | MEDIUM — admin handler attends, talks to vendors |

**The expert-comptable angle is particularly strong in France:** French experts-comptables (accountants) serve micro-enterprises and artisans. They often install and configure software for their clients. If an expert-comptable adopts a tool and recommends it to 20 artisan clients, that's a single sales motion with 20 conversions. This is how Pennylane grew significantly in the French market.

---

## Counterpoint 5: The Buyer-User Split Changes the Free Tier Calculus

**D6/D46 assumption:** Conversion happens when the artisan (Marc) hits the Free limit or feels the product's value.

**The counter:** If the admin handler is the user, the conversion trigger changes:

- The admin handler is evaluating the tool for effectiveness, not experiencing "admin pain"
- They will assess: does this save me time? Is it faster than my current system (Excel/Word)?
- Conversion is triggered by: demonstrable time savings, professional output, integration with accounting software
- The "limit hit" may not trigger them at all — if they have 8 clients and the Free limit is 10, they may simply not use the product beyond 5-6 clients because "it works fine for my needs"

**The implication:** Conversion optimization should focus on the admin handler's evaluation criteria (efficiency, time savings, output quality) rather than Marc's limit anxiety.

---

## What D3 Gets Right (And Should Be Retained)

- The solo artisan persona is real and important — especially for the buying decision
- Admin pain is the trigger — but the pain is experienced differently by buyer vs user
- Simplicity-first is correct — admin handlers are evaluating multiple tools and simplicity reduces evaluation friction

---

## Verdict

**D3 is PARTIALLY REVERSED.** Marc remains the primary buyer and final decision-maker. But the GTM must account for the admin handler as the primary evaluator and user.

**New GTM priority recommendations:**
1. **Add expert-comptable referral channel** — single motion → multiple artisan conversions
2. **Optimize for comparison site presence** — admin handlers search, not browse WhatsApp
3. **Reframe messaging for admin handler pain** — operational efficiency ("gagnez 2h/semaine") rather than emotional/forgetfulness framing
4. **Retain WhatsApp/artisan groups** — reaches the buyer (artisan) for endorsement, not the evaluator

**The single most important GTM insight from this debate:** The expert-comptable referral channel is severely underutilized. An expert-comptable with 50 artisan clients is a channel, not just a customer.
