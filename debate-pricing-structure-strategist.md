# Product Strategist Position Paper: Usage-Based Pricing for Mini-CRM

**Topic:** Flat-rate (Free + €29/mo) vs. Usage-based pricing for French artisans
**Author:** Product Strategist (Challenger Position)
**Date:** 2026-03-30

---

## Executive Summary

The current flat-rate decision (Free + €29/month Pro) is optimized for SaaS elegance, not artisan reality. For Marc—the 50-year-old French electrician with lumpy, seasonal cash flows—paying €29 in January when he invoiced €0 creates psychological and financial friction that usage-based pricing eliminates. This paper presents 10 arguments for usage-based pricing and challenges the core assumption behind D70/D71.

---

## The 10-Point Case for Usage-Based Pricing

### 1. **French BTP Seasonality Is Documented and Severe**

The French construction sector (BTP) experiences a 20-30% revenue drop in January-February compared to the April-September peak. For a solo artisan electrician doing renovation work (not new construction), winter months still see reduced activity: fewer client meetings, delayed project starts, and general year-end slowdown in spending decisions. The INSEE (French national statistics office) data shows construction sector activity indexes consistently dip below 90 (base 100) from December through February.

**The implication:** Marc may earn €0 to €800 in January. A €29 fixed cost feels proportionally enormous when income is near zero. €29 on €0 revenue is infinite CAC. €29 on €5,000 revenue is negligible.

---

### 2. **The Psychology of Paying for Nothing**

Flat-rate subscriptions create a cognitive dissonance problem: *"I'm paying to manage clients I'm not currently earning from."* This isn't rational—Marc knows he needs the tool year-round—but it's emotionally real. Behavioral economics (Kahneman's prospect theory) shows humans weight losses during low-income periods more heavily. Paying €29 in January when you invoiced nothing feels like a loss on top of a loss.

Usage-based pricing eliminates this. If Marc sends 5 devis in January, he pays €5-10. If he sends none, he pays nothing. The emotional framing shifts from "paying for a service I'm not using" to "paying for value I received."

---

### 3. **Flat-Rate Creates the Churn It Claims to Prevent**

D70/D71 argued flat-rate reduces churn because "simpler." But this logic inverts when cash is tight. Consider:

- January: Marc hesitates to pay €29. He's busy, invoicing is slow, the tool feels less urgent.
- February: Same problem. He's now 2 months invested and considering cancellation.
- March: He cancels, reasoning "I'll come back when work picks up."

**Result:** Churn during the low season, before the high season when he'd actually use the tool most.

Usage-based prevents this entirely. There's nothing to cancel. Marc drifts in and out naturally, paying only when active. No cancellation friction. No "I'll come back later" promise that rarely materializes.

---

### 4. **Usage-Based Aligns Price with Value Delivery—Literally**

The core value prop of Mini-CRM for Marc is: *"Help me get paid faster, track my devis, chase late payers."* These are income-generating activities. When Marc invoiced €5,000 last month, the relance automation and financial snapshot delivered real value. When he invoiced €200, value was limited.

Pricing at €29/month decouples payment from value. Usage-based re-couples them. You're not charging for the software—you're charging for the financial outcomes you're creating. This is value-based pricing, not just usage-based pricing.

---

### 5. **Lower Entry Friction = Higher Funnel Conversion**

A free tier + €29/month creates a psychological commitment ladder:
1. Free (no risk)
2. €29/month (commitment decision)

The step from free to paid is a €29/month decision. This requires Marc to:
- Believe the tool will return >€29/month in value
- Plan to use it actively for at least 1-2 months
- Have €29 available in his cash flow

Any one of these fails = no conversion.

Usage-based reduces the conversion trigger to a €1-2 decision per action. "Should I send this devis through the app?" is an easier yes than "Should I commit to €29/month?" Conversion rates at micro-transactions are demonstrably higher than subscription commitments for sporadic-use cases.

---

### 6. **The "Simpler" Argument Assumes Marc Thinks Like a SaaS Buyer**

D70/D71 concluded flat-rate is simpler. But this conclusion comes from a SaaS product perspective—"simpler to understand, simpler to forecast." Marc doesn't think in SaaS terms. He's an electrician. He thinks in project terms, in devis terms, in "I got paid that job" terms.

For Marc, usage-based (pay per devis sent, capped at €29) is *more intuitive* than a monthly subscription because it maps to his mental model: *"I pay for what I use."* This is the pricing model he understands from every other service in his life—plumber by the hour, materials by the unit.

The "simplicity" argument confuses product-team simplicity (one subscription to manage) with customer simplicity (what am I actually paying for?).

---

### 7. **January Is the Perfect Storm for Subscription Fatigue**

French artisan cash flow has a specific pattern:
- **December:** Big expenses (Christmas, taxes, social charges)
- **January:** Slow activity, sometimes no work for 2-3 weeks post-holidays
- **February:** Gradual pickup, but still slow
- **March/April:** Activity returns

January is when French households and businesses are cash-constrained. Asking Marc to pay €29 in this window is asking during his worst financial moment. Usage-based sidesteps this entirely—there's no invoice in January because there was no activity.

---

### 8. **Usage-Based Creates a Natural Seasonal Buffer**

With flat-rate, the slow season (Jan-Feb) is when you're most likely to lose Marc. With usage-based:
- Low season = low/no payment (no friction)
- High season = active usage, higher payment, higher perceived value

The tool pays for itself when Marc is earning, which is exactly when he has cash to pay. This creates a self-reinforcing cycle: better cash flow month → more reason to use the tool → better cash flow next month.

---

### 9. **Usage-Based With a Cap Retains the Price Ceiling**

A key D70/D71 concern was predictability—"Marc needs to know his max cost." Usage-based pricing at €1-2 per devis, capped at €29/month, addresses this directly:
- Marc knows he'll never pay more than €29
- His max cost equals the flat rate
- His average cost will be *lower* than flat-rate in low seasons

This isn't a compromise—it's the best of both worlds. Predictability preserved. Seasonal friction eliminated.

---

### 10. **Evidence from Comparable Markets: Field Service Management**

The UK and US markets for field service/trades CRM (Housecall Pro, Workiz, Jobber) have moved toward usage-based and hybrid models specifically because of the seasonal/sporadic use case. Jobber's data (publicly cited) shows higher annual retention with hybrid vs. flat subscription. The reasoning: customers who pay during active periods build habit and trust, returning the next season. Customers on flat subscriptions who have a slow Q1 often cancel before Q2 recovery.

---

## Challenging the D70/D71 Conclusions Directly

**D70 claimed:** "Flat monthly rate is simpler and aligns with how French SMBs buy SaaS."

**Response:**
1. "Simpler" is undefined. Simpler for whom? The product team or the customer? For Marc, "pay per devis" is simpler than "pay monthly regardless."
2. "How French SMBs buy SaaS" assumes French artisans buy SaaS regularly. Most don't. They're not SaaS-native buyers. The "French SMB buys SaaS the way everyone else does" assumption ignores that most French artisans (average age 45-55) have limited SaaS experience and default to consumption-based mental models.

**D71 claimed:** "Flat rate is less churn-prone."

**Response:**
This is only true if the customer maintains steady usage. For sporadic, seasonal users, flat-rate churn risk is *highest during the exact months when the product is least valuable to them.* Usage-based eliminates seasonal churn entirely because there's nothing to churn—payment is tied to activity, not to time.

---

## VERDICT

**Usage-based pricing (per devis sent, capped at €29/month) outperforms flat-rate for this product and persona.**

The flat-rate model optimizes for product-team simplicity and maximum revenue per active user—but it creates structural friction for seasonal artisans that usage-based eliminates entirely. Marc's January cash flow reality isn't an edge case; it's the core use case. Pricing should reflect that.

**Recommended model:**
- **Free tier:** Create account, manage up to 5 clients, basic tracking
- **Pro tier:** €1.50 per devis sent (or €2 per facture issued), capped at €29/month
- **Conversion trigger:** First paid devis/facture through the platform (maintains the financial milestone gate)

This preserves revenue ceiling predictability, aligns payment with value delivered, eliminates seasonal friction, and converts better in low-cash months. The €29 becomes a natural ceiling Marc reaches during active months—not a floor he pays during slow ones.

**Flat-rate is a relic of SaaS assumptions applied to a non-SaaS customer. Usage-based is the honest pricing model for value that's actually delivered.**
