## The Problem

> "5 beta users (real artisans, not the team) complete client → devis → WhatsApp PDF send without assistance."

The debate log has **never challenged where these 5 beta users come from**. The action item appended to the Sprint 0 verdict says:

This assumption is wrong. It needs to be challenged before Sprint 0 begins, or Louis will spend 6.5 days building and then discover he has nobody to test it on.

## Challenge 1: The "Gabin/Maël Network" Assumption Is Unfounded

Louis is a web developer intern at Grinto. He is co-founder of Kuroba studio with Gabin (backend developer) and Maëli (designer). Their professional networks are:

- **Louis:** Grinto colleagues (web developers), Kuroba clients (if any — landing page not live), developer community
- **Gabin:** developers, likely computer science background, family connections unknown
- **Maëli:** designers, creative industry — graphic designers, studios, agencies

None of these networks are naturally populated with French artisans (electricians, plumbers, tilers, HVAC installers). The 04:49 Sprint 0 verdict appends "Select artisans in Gabin/Maël's network" as if this is a solved problem. It is not.

**The risk:** Louis spends Sprint 0 building → Sprint 0 ends → hunts for 5 beta users → discovers his entire network is developers and designers → recruits 3 friends who installed the app out of loyalty → gets polite but useless feedback → ships a product nobody asked for.

---

## Challenge 2: The Guerrilla Test at the Wholesaler Was Not Beta Recruitment

The U15 guerrilla usability test (5 artisans at Point P or Gedimat on a Saturday morning) was designed for **usability validation** — watching whether Marc understands the UI, where his fingers hesitate, what language he uses. It was never designed as a **beta user recruitment pipeline**.

There is a critical difference:
- **Guerrilla test:** One-time, 10 minutes, single task, no ongoing relationship
- **Beta user:** Installs the app, uses it over days/weeks, completes the full flow, provides honest feedback

You cannot convert a guerrilla test subject into a beta user without a second ask, a follow-up relationship, and an ongoing commitment. The debate log treats these as equivalent. They are not.

---

## Challenge 3: Expert-Comptable Route Is Week 3 — Too Late

D72/D139 resolved: Week 1 = problem-framing with Louis's own accountant (no product demo). Week 3 = product demo with 2-3 external expert-comptables.

**This is correct for product validation but wrong for beta user recruitment.** The debate log treats these as the same conversation. They are not:

- **Problem-framing conversation (Week 1):** "What do artisan clients consistently get wrong? What pain points do you observe?" — useful for schema design
- **Beta user recruitment (now):** "Would you introduce me to 2-3 artisan clients who might test a new devis/facture tool I'm building?" — different ask, different timing

The expert-comptable route is NOT a Week 3 conversation. Louis's own expert-comptable can make warm introductions to artisan clients THIS WEEK, before any product exists, as a pure favor.

## The Argument: Louis's Expert-Comptable Is the Beta Acquisition Channel

**Position: Louis's own expert-comptable is the fastest, highest-quality beta user acquisition channel available this week.**

Why this channel works:

1. **Warm relationship.** Louis already has an established relationship with his own expert-comptable. This is not cold outreach. The ask ("could you introduce me to 2-3 artisan clients who might test something I'm building?") is low-stakes for the accountant and high-value for Louis.

2. **Validated quality.** An expert-comptable who serves artisan clients has already done the filtering: these are real artisans with real businesses, not friends-of-friends who will tell Louis what he wants to hear.

3. **Compliance-aware users.** Artisans who work with an expert-comptable are already used to professional document standards. Their feedback on PDF quality and mentions légales will be genuinely挑剔 — not polite tolerance.

4. **Speed.** This can happen THIS WEEK, before Sprint 0 begins. Louis calls his accountant, asks for introductions, and has 2-3 warm leads by Wednesday.

5. **No conflict of interest.** D72/D139 correctly identified Louis's own accountant cannot validate the product (they're paid by Louis = conflict). But they CAN make introductions to their clients. These are separate roles.

**Why NOT other channels:**

- **Gabin/Maël's network:** Likely developers and designers, not tradespeople. Requires asking and likely discovering nobody suitable.
- **Grinto's clients:** Unlikely to be artisans — Grinto appears to be a web studio. Louis would need to ask, wait, and likely find none.
- **Wholesaler guerrilla approach:** One-time usability test, not beta recruitment. Cannot convert to ongoing beta use without a second ask and relationship continuity.
- **CCI/artisans' guilds:** Cold outreach. Low response rate. Week 3+ timeline. Louis doesn't have a warm intro to these organizations.
- **Facebook/LinkedIn groups:** Cold posting. Low signal. Response rate uncertain. Timing unpredictable.

## Recommended Beta Acquisition Channel: Expert-Comptable Warm Intro

**Primary:** Louis calls his expert-comptable this week. Asks for introductions to 2-3 artisan clients who might be open to testing a new tool. NOT a product demo — a relationship introduction.

If yes, one intro per person. Maëli too — designers often work with small businesses, she may know an artisan client.

## What NOT To Do

- **Do not recruit friends who are not artisans.** Friends give friendly feedback. They tell you what you want to hear. They don't represent the target persona. A friend who is a web developer but pretends to be an artisan for 30 minutes is worse than no beta user — he gives false confidence.
- **Do not wait until Sprint 0 ends to find beta users.** Building without a testing pipeline is planning to fail.
- **Do not conflate "network audit" with "network available."** Asking Gabin "do you know any artisans?" may produce a polite "I'll ask around" which produces nothing by Friday. The question must be specific and the answer must be documented same-day.
- **Do not send a cold email to a wholesaler counter staff.** Point P and Gedimat staff are not the target. The guerrilla test was designed for Saturday morning walk-ins, not B2B outreach.
