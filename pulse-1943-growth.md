# CHALLENGE: iOS-First Is the Wrong Default for Mini-CRM

**Pulse 19:43 | Role: Growth Strategist**

---

## The Problem with Assumptions

D92 resolved to go iOS-first with Android in Month 2, validated by a geo-targeted poll in Week 1. The logic: iOS users skew business, Android moderation risk, Expo iOS builds more reliable.

This is **tech-elite bias dressed up as strategy**.

Let me break it down.

---

## Argument 1: You're Targeting the Wrong Persona

Marc is a **50-year-old French electrician**. He wakes up at 6:30, drives to job sites, manages quotes on his phone between appointments. He's been in the trades for 25 years. He owns a Samsung or Xiaomi — not because he's cheap, but because **Android is what working people use**.

The French artisan/construction market skews Android. This isn't anecdotal — it's demographics. Construction, electrical, plumbing: these are working-class trades. The iPhone is not the default. If you're building for Marc and assuming he'll have an iPhone, you're already building for the wrong user.

Defaulting to iOS-first means **defaulting to the segment that is NOT your primary buyer**.

---

## Argument 2: The "Business User on iOS" Assumption Is Lazy

Yes, iOS has higher average revenue per user in B2C consumer apps. But this is B2B for tradespeople. You're not selling to startup founders. You're selling to a 52-year-old plumber who just upgraded from a Nokia flip phone to a Xiaomi.

The "iOS = serious business user" heuristic is a Silicon Valley myth that does not survive contact with French rural demographics. Apply the actual persona.

---

## Argument 3: The Play Store Moderation "Risk" Is Overblown

The debate cited Android Play Store moderation as a risk factor for B2B finance apps. 

Let's be precise: a legitimate app with proper legal documentation (SIRET, legal mentions, CGV) gets reviewed in **24-72 hours**. This is a delay, not a blocker. Apple's App Store review, by contrast, is slower, more arbitrary, and more likely to reject B2B finance apps on subjective grounds.

If anything, **Android has the lower friction path to market**.

---

## Argument 4: Direct Distribution Is a Feature

Android APK installation allows **direct distribution** without App Store gatekeeping. You can distribute via your website, via prescriber networks, via USB stick at a trade show. You're not dependent on Apple's approval for every update.

For a B2B tool targeting risk-averse older users, being able to say "here's the APK, install it" is not a workaround — it's a legitimate distribution channel.

---

## What Should Happen

The geo-targeted poll in Week 1 is the right instinct. **But the default should be Android-first, not iOS-first.**

Run the poll. If the data says iOS, great — go iOS. But starting on iOS when your persona skews Android is starting with the wrong foot.

---

## Bottom Line

iOS-first for Mini-CRM is: 

- Wrong persona assumption
- Lazy "business = Apple" stereotype
- Overblown moderation risk
- Ignoring a higher-penetration addressable market

**Reverse the default. Android-first. Let the data confirm or deny. Don't start from a Silicon Valley assumption about who "serious business users" are.**
