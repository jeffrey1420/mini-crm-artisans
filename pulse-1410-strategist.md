# STRATEGIST POSITION: D53 — Landing Page Framing

## Core Argument

Both sides of this debate are arguing about what *claim* to make — simplicity versus professionalism. They're asking the wrong question. The real question is: **what belief must a prospect hold before the landing page can convert them, and what can we show them (not tell them) that creates that belief?**

"Sans vous prendre la tête" is not a simplicity *claim* — it's a trust signal that says "we built this for someone like you." The objection it handles isn't "will this be easy to learn?" (the defense's argument). It's deeper: "is this made for people who have real businesses, or for people who barely know how to use a smartphone?" The phrase answers that. The challenger's mistake is reading it as infantilizing when it's actually *inclusive of competence*. The defense's mistake is defending the wrong benefit. This phrase doesn't prove the product is easy — it proves the product was made for a serious professional who doesn't have time for BS.

The challenger is right that "professional-grade" is an unsubstantiated claim at €29. But their alternative — "Votre entreprise, au complet" — is ALSO a claim. Neither wins without proof. The actual resolution: **move from claims to demonstration**. "Vos devis en 5 minutes, sans vous prendre la tête" makes the headline testable in the first session — a prospect can verify "did I create and send a devis in 5 minutes?" faster than they can verify "does this look professional?" Simplicity framed as a *demonstrable fact* beats either claim.

## Assumption Challenged

**The assumption being challenged:** That "Sans vous prendre la tête" talks down to a 45-55yo who ran a professional business for 15-25 years. This is wrong because it misreads what that phrase signals.

The challenger reads "sans vous prendre la tête" as: "this is a simple tool for people who find software hard." But that's not what the phrase communicates to its audience. A 50-year-old artisan hearing it interprets it as: "this was made by people who understand my time is valuable and my patience is limited — they're not going to waste my time with a complex setup." It's a signal of product empathy, not product simplicity. It says "we know what your time is worth" — which is precisely what a competent professional wants to hear, not "we think you need something easy."

The deeper error: assuming that 45-55yo artisans want to be addressed as seasoned professionals first. They do. But "sans vous prendre la tête" IS addressing them as seasoned professionals — it's saying "you've earned the right to tools that don't waste your time." That's professional respect, not condescension.

## Resolution

**D53 resolves to: Testimonial-anchored simplicity as demonstrable fact, not as claim.**

Headline: *"Vos devis et factures, sans vous prendre la tête."* — RETAINED, but the subheadline must make it *demonstrable* not *promised*.

Subheadline: *"Créez et envoyez votre premier devis en 5 minutes. Depuis votre téléphone."*

This is the synthesis neither side offered:
- The simplicity camp's insight (WhatsApp signal, objection-killer) is preserved in the headline
- The challenger camp's insight (claims without proof are empty) is addressed by the subheadline making the 5-minute claim *specific and testable*
- Neither "professional-grade" nor "entreprise au complet" appears in the hero — those are claims requiring proof the landing page can't provide
- The proof lives *inside the product* (the free tier, the first devis flow), not on the landing page

**What the hero does NOT say:** No mention of relances, no mention of professionalism, no mention of "entreprise." These are conversion-unproven claims that add cognitive load without creating belief.

**Landing page hero structure:**
- **H1:** Vos devis et factures, sans vous prendre la tête.
- **H2:** Créez et envoyez votre premier devis en 5 minutes. Depuis votre téléphone.
- **CTA:** Commencer gratuit — zero commitment, no credit card
- **Below fold:** 3 specific features (client file, devis creation, factures), each with a one-line concrete benefit — not a claim, a fact

## Action Items

1. **Landing page copy update:** H1 stays "Sans vous prendre la tête." H2 becomes the 5-minute specific claim. No "professional-grade" language in hero.
2. **Free tier as proof mechanism:** The landing page CTA leads to Free tier. The trial IS the proof. If the product delivers "5-minute devis" and the Free tier demonstrates it, no claim on the landing page needs to be believed — it can be verified.
3. **A/B test deferred:** No A/B test until guerrilla test confirms which headline variant resonates with actual artisans. The 5-minute subheadline is the priority test.
4. **Remove "Votre entreprise, au complet" from consideration** — too abstract, no proof path on landing page, and it recycles the challenger camp's unsubstantiated claim.
