## Debate PS-D152: Eliminate Annual Billing Entirely — Monthly €29 Only at Launch

### Product Strategist — Kill D138's Annual Option. Monthly €29. No Exceptions at v1.

**Assumption challenged:** "Annual billing solves seasonality for cash-flow-sensitive artisans" (D138 and supporting debates). This assumption is false. Annual billing *masks* seasonality — it does not solve it. It converts a temporary churn signal into a permanent customer loss.

---

**Core arguments:**

1. **Annual billing hides the wrong problem.** If Marc prepays €240 and then cancels in month 8 during a slow season, Louis doesn't see "this artisan couldn't justify €29/month in a slow period." He sees "I lost a prepaid customer at renewal." The underlying problem — insufficient perceived value during low-activity months — is masked by the billing structure. Monthly exposes this signal clearly. Annual eliminates it from view. Louis cannot fix what he can't see.

2. **Annual at launch is a second SKU before product-market fit.** Every SKU adds: separate pricing page copy, separate onboarding flow, separate churn/renewal logic, separate accounting treatment, separate LTV calculations, separate dunning sequences. These are all premature at v1. Louis needs clean, simple signal. One SKU. One churn metric. One story. Introducing annual billing before monthly churn is understood is premature optimization of revenue at the expense of product clarity.

3. **"Annual-first" framing poisons monthly tier perception, even as opt-in.** The very presence of an annual option changes how users evaluate monthly. "Why would I pay €29/month when I could pay €20/month?" shifts the mental frame from "is €29/month worth it?" to "am I being ripped off by paying monthly?" This is the anchoring effect in action. Remove the annual option, and the only question is whether €29/month delivers value — which is the correct question for v1.

4. **The "sovereignty over billing cycle" framing (D138) is circular.** D138 argued artisans should have the choice between monthly (flexibility) and annual (cost savings). But the same argument that justifies annual at launch would justify removing monthly entirely — "artisans who want flexibility can cancel and rejoin." Both positions use "sovereignty" to justify opposite choices. The honest framing: monthly is the honest tier because it measures genuine product value each month. Annual is a revenue acceleration tactic for products that have already proven value.

5. **€240 upfront IS expensive for a solo artisan, regardless of the monthly equivalent.** The "it's only €20/month!" framing is a marketing trick, not an empathy signal. For a solo plumber in January — between jobs, heating bills high, income low — €240 is a significant cash outlay. Telling them "but it's just €20/month equivalent!" doesn't reduce the cash drain. Monthly €29 is the empathetic choice because it matches cash flow to value received, not to a calendar.

6. **Louis's real v1 question: do artisans find the product valuable enough to stay monthly?** If monthly churn is low AND activation is high, annual billing gets introduced in v1.2 as an upsell to *retained users who have already answered that question*. Annual at launch asks the question before the product has had a chance to answer it.

---

**Verdict:** D138 should be **ELIMINATED**, not revised. Monthly €29 ONLY at launch. No annual option. This gives Louis the cleanest possible signal on product-market fit: monthly retention rate. Annual billing, if introduced at all, is a v1.2 decision — introduced as an upsell to users who have already demonstrated monthly commitment, not as a launch-day choice.
