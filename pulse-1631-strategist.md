# STRATEGIST POSITION: Challenge D63 — The "Situation Financière" Snapshot Is the Wrong Primary Pull

## The Assumption Being Challenged

D63 resolved that the **"situation financière" snapshot** — automatically produced weekly, surfaced as the Free tier home screen — is the Free tier's primary value output. The rationale: it's output the artisan *wants* to consume, answering "how is my business right now?" This assumption is wrong. It misidentifies what Marc values and positions the product in direct competition with Pennylane and Indy — the two tools he already rejected.

## Problem 1: Accounting Tool Positioning

Pennylane and Indy built their brand around the financial snapshot. Their pitch: "We automatically track your finances." By making the financial snapshot our Free tier's primary output, we're borrowing Pennylane's core value proposition at a lower price point. That's comparison, not differentiation. Pennylane wins on their own turf.

**We can out-devis Pennylane.** Our differentiation is devis management — the document, the communication, the professional artifact. The snapshot pulls toward an accounting summary. But pull toward *what* — a beautifully formatted devis that makes Marc look more professional?

## Problem 2: Marc Is Never Near His Phone When It Matters

The snapshot assumes Marc will open it. But Marc is an electrician — on job sites, under sinks, checking his phone in 30-second windows. A weekly financial digest doesn't fit that moment. It assumes he has time to sit down, review outstanding devis, compare revenue to last month, and act. He doesn't.

Pennylane owns the Sunday-evening review moment. We have 30 seconds in a van.

## The Real Pull: The Devis Document Itself

The Free tier's actual hook isn't a business summary — it's the *experience of creating and sending a professional devis*. That devis is a physical artifact. It goes to his client. It sits on the client's kitchen table next to three competitor devis. If his devis looks more professional — cleaner layout, better typography, clearer line items — he wins the job.

Pennylane doesn't compete in that moment. Indy doesn't compete in that moment. Only we do.

D63 confused "output we can generate automatically" with "output that creates upgrade desire." What makes Marc want Pro: "My competitors' devis look like Word documents from 2005. Mine looks like it came from a real business."

## The Alternative: Document Quality as Primary Pull

**Swap the snapshot for the devis preview.**

Instead of a financial summary on the Free tier home screen, surface a **"your recent devis"** view — showing the last devis he sent, formatted exactly as the client saw it. The visual is the devis itself, not a graph. The message: "This is what your clients see."

Clean typography. Clear line items. A layout that communicates competence before the client reads a single word. That artifact travels outside the app, gets forwarded to a spouse for approval, sits in an inbox for three days before a decision.

A financial snapshot is consumed once, by Marc, in a review moment. A devis is consumed multiple times, by clients, in decision moments. **The devis has more reach and more differentiation value.**

The "situation financière" can still exist — as a secondary tab, buried in settings. But it should not be the Free tier's primary value output or home screen.

## What This Changes

- **Free tier home screen:** Recent devis preview instead of financial snapshot
- **Upgrade trigger:** "Your devis looks great — imagine adding your logo and reminders" at devis #3
- **Pennylane differentiation:** We win on document quality; they win on financial depth. Don't compete on their turf.

## Summary

D63 confused "self-generating pull" with "accounting software positioning." The snapshot pulls toward Pennylane's feature. The devis preview pulls toward ours. Make the document the hero.
