# Debate: Evening-Onboarding Conflict (D83 vs D93)

## The Contradiction

**D83/D88/D89's position:** Event-only notifications. Any fixed-time or configurable time-window notification (including 8pm "soir ritual") was rejected because it "assumes a daily rhythm that doesn't universally exist for French artisans." The configurable notification window was KILLED entirely.

**D93/D95's position:** Guided Creation Flow must be "evening-only, 10-15 minutes" because a 5-minute daytime task "assumes a focused attention moment that doesn't exist." Day 1 is structured as: daytime orientation + **evening Guided Creation**.

**Why they're inconsistent:** D83 says "don't assume the artisan has evening availability for anything" — then D93 says "but the Guided Creation Flow requires exactly that evening focused moment." These positions are mutually exclusive. You cannot reject evening assumptions for notifications while requiring evening commitment for onboarding.

The debate log flagged this but never resolved it.

---

## Evidence and Arguments

### For D83's Position (No Fixed Evening Ritual)

1. **Unpredictable work schedules:** French artisans (particularly in construction, renovation, trades) often work non-standard hours — job sites start early, physical labor ends the day exhausted, seasonal crunch changes everything. Assuming 8pm availability is presumptuous.

2. **Notification fatigue:** Push notifications at arbitrary times get ignored or uninstalled. The configurable window was KILLED because it still assumed the system knew better than the user when to intrude.

3. **Event-driven > time-driven:** First accepted devis is a genuine triggering event worth a notification. "It's 8pm on a Tuesday" is not. The notification should serve the artisan's business milestone, not a calendar.

4. **Cognitive load at wrong time:** Evening for many artisans is family dinner, decompression, or second jobs (admin, estimates). Interrupting this with a "do your CRM setup now" notification is tone-deaf.

5. **Configurable window was KILLED for a reason:** D89 explicitly rejected the configurable notification window — meaning the team already decided: **do not schedule things for users**.

---

### For D93's Position (Evening Guided Creation)

1. **The flow is opt-in intent, not imposed interruption:** An artisan who completes daytime Day 1 orientation has already signaled intent to continue. Asking them to block 10-15 minutes that evening is different from a random 8pm push notification to someone who hasn't engaged.

2. **Guided flow requires sustained focus, not just attention:** D95's insight is correct — 5 minutes is insufficient for a proper Guided Creation. Unlike a quick notification read, this requires the artisan to sit down, think, create content. That requires a different time context than "checking a notification."

3. **Evening aligns with actual creative windows:** Many artisans do admin/estimates in the evening after kids sleep or job sites close. 10-15 minutes post-dinner is a realistic creative window, even if a 9am notification is not.

4. **The alternative (daytime 5-min assumption) was already rejected:** D95 explicitly said "5-minute daytime task assumes a focused attention moment that doesn't exist." Evening 10-15 min was the fallback resolution. You can't reject daytime AND evening.

5. **Onboarding ritual creates habit formation:** Evening onboarding (like Duolingo's model) is a known pattern for building habits. A structured evening session creates a committed ritual, not an interrupted one.

---

## The Resolution

**The contradiction dissolves when you separate "notification" from "commitment."**

D83's rejection of 8pm notifications is correct. D93's requirement for evening Guided Creation is also correct. They address different problems:

- **Notification:** Interrupt the user only on genuine business events (first accepted devis). Do not assume a daily rhythm.
- **Guided Creation Flow:** When the user has **already chosen** to start onboarding, ask them to commit to a focused evening session. This is opt-in commitment, not imposed interruption.

The key distinction is **intent and consent:**
- A push notification at 8pm assumes the user is available — D83 correctly rejected this.
- An evening slot the user selects during onboarding is the user telling us when they're available — D93 correctly captures this.

**Resolution:** D83/D88/D89 govern **reactive notifications**. D93/D95 govern **proactive onboarding commitments**. These operate in different interaction contexts and are not contradictory when properly scoped.

---

## Specific Decisions

1. **Notifications remain event-only (D83/D89 decision stands):** First accepted devis triggers the notification. No time-based or configurable-window notifications. Ever.

2. **Guided Creation Flow is offered at a time the artisan chooses:** During Day 1 orientation, present the Guided Creation Flow as "book your 10-15 minute session." Let the artisan select from available slots — including evening options, or next-day morning options. Do not force evening.

3. **If no slot selected, default to async/self-paced:** If the artisan doesn't book a session, the Guided Creation Flow is available async — they complete it whenever they have 10-15 minutes of focused time. This respects D83's concern while honoring D93's insight that it needs more than 5 minutes.

4. **Timezone-aware slot suggestions:** When presenting slot options, use the artisan's timezone (D88's timezone awareness principle applies here, but as a helpful default, not a forced notification).

5. **Remove "evening-only" as a constraint — replace with "focused session":** D93 should be reframed: Guided Creation requires a 10-15 minute focused window, which most artisans find in the evening, but this is an observation about when the window typically exists, not a mandate that it must be evening.

6. **D95's 5-minute rejection stands:** The Guided Creation Flow cannot be a 5-minute task. If an async/split approach is needed to make it achievable in shorter bursts, that architecture should be explored — but the flow itself is not a quick notification check.

---

**Bottom line:** The contradiction was real but the resolution is clean. D83 governs how we interrupt users (don't). D93 governs how we onboard users (with their consent and chosen timing). These are separate decisions for separate interaction types.**
