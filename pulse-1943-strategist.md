## Challenging: The "First Sent Devis" Conversion Trigger

The Growth Strategist wants to flip the trigger from "first accepted devis" to "first sent devis." I understand the logic — the acceptance chain breaks too often. But the cure is worse than the disease.

**"First sent devis" fires the moment Marc clicks a button.** That's not a moment of value realization. That's the first step of a workflow that may go nowhere. His devis could sit unanswered for a week. His client could ghost him. He could be sending devis to his three regular clients who already know the price verbally and will never open the email. At that exact moment — clicked send — nothing has happened yet. The product has not solved a problem. It has started a process.

And we're going to ask him for €29? No.

---

**The trigger should be "first paid facture."**

This is the moment the product actually delivers. Marc created the invoice. He sent it. His client paid it. Money arrived in his account. The full loop closed. *That's* when Marc thinks "this tool works." Not "this tool let me type some numbers into a PDF." Not "this tool emailed someone who may or may not respond." Paid. In his account. Real value, tangible, earned.

From a conversion psychology standpoint, this is obvious. You convert people when they're holding the proof of value in their hands — not when they're hoping for it. "First paid facture" means the artisan has already experienced the core promise of the product: stop chasing money manually, get paid. He's a candidate for payment the *after* that moment, when the feeling is fresh and attributable.

---

**The deeper flaw in the debate: treating conversion as a notification.**

Both "first accepted devis" and "first sent devis" share the same assumption: the software detects an event and fires a push notification or in-app prompt. Hey Marc, time to pay! Click here!

This is not how you sell to a 50-year-old artisan.

These are people who respond to relationships, not algorithms. Who distrust apps that ask for money. Who will simply stop using a tool that nags them. The conversion moment for this persona is a *conversation*, not a trigger.

**The better model: 14 days free, then Louis on WhatsApp.**

Day 1–14: Marc uses the product freely. Creates devis. Sends them. Maybe gets paid, maybe doesn't. No pressure. No nagging.

Day 14: Louis (a real human, not a bot) sends a WhatsApp. Not a sales pitch. A check-in.

> *"Salut Marc, comment ça se passe ? Tu as pu tester un peu l'outil ? Des questions, des bloqueurs ? Je peux te montrer deux-trois trucs qui pourraient te faire gagner du temps."*

This is how artisans buy. They buy from people they trust, who check on them, who don't make them feel like a conversion funnel. The WhatsApp conversation — human, casual, helpful — is the actual conversion trigger. The "first paid facture" milestone just tells Louis *when* to make that call.

**Summary:**
- "First sent devis" → too early, no value proven, alienates the user
- "First paid facture" → right moment, value confirmed, clear attribution
- Push notification conversion → wrong model entirely for this persona
- Human WhatsApp check-in at Day 14 → how you actually close French artisans
