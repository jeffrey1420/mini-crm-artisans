# Debate 18: Dashboard-First Home View vs. Client Timeline

**Position:** The home view must be a Dashboard — not a Client Timeline.

**Premise accepted from Debate 14:** Kanban lives in a secondary Jobs tab.
**Premise being challenged:** Client Timeline as the home view.

---

## The Core Argument: Habits Win Retention, Not Archives

At €29/month, Mini-CRM has no enterprise-grade lock-in. The only durable retention moat is habit formation — specifically, the daily ritual of opening the app first thing in the morning. If we lose that ritual, we lose the subscription.

**The user's morning is the battlefield.** And the morning belongs to the Dashboard.

---

## Why Marc Opens His Phone Before Jobs

Marc the plumber wakes up at 6:30 AM. He has 12 minutes. He doesn't open his phone to browse a reverse-chronological feed of everything that happened with Client X — he opens it to answer: *"What do I need to do today?"*

A Dashboard answers that question instantly:

- **"À suivre"** — Clients you've tagged for follow-up
- **"Relances dues"** — Invoices overdue, ready to send
- **"Devis en attente"** — Quotes awaiting response

This is a command center. It tells Marc what to do before he's even finished his coffee.

The Client Timeline, by contrast, is an archive. It's a ledger of what happened — not a map of what needs to happen. It rewards curiosity, not action. And Marc, at 6:30 AM with 12 minutes, is not curious. He's operational.

---

## Timeline Is Passive. Dashboard Is Active.

A reverse-chronological feed per client is a read mode. It tells stories about the past. The Dashboard is write mode — it creates the next action.

**Passive tools get opened when something is wrong.** Active tools get opened first, to prevent something from going wrong.

At €29/month, we cannot afford to be opened only when things break. We must be opened *proactively*, as part of a daily workflow. That only happens if the home screen is action-generating.

---

## Daily Habit Formation Is the Only Durable Moat

Consumer SaaS retention research (from Duolingo, Headspace, Notion's free tier studies) is consistent: **the first 3 seconds in the app determine whether the habit forms.** If the user sees a feed, they browse. If they see a command center, they act. Acting creates context. Context creates value. Value creates tomorrow's return.

The Dashboard creates a loop:
> Open → See action → Do action → See result → Return tomorrow

The Timeline creates a different loop:
> Open → Browse past → Maybe act → Close → Forget to return

One of these loops is a subscription renewal. The other is churn wearing a smile.

---

## The Growth Strategist's Earlier Position (Debate 16 Addendum)

This is not a new argument. The Growth Strategist proposed Dashboard-first in Debate 16 as the retention-optimal structure. Debate 14's resolution to make Client Timeline the home view prioritized the *information architecture* of a CRM over the *behavioral architecture* of a daily tool. These are different products. Mini-CRM must be both — and for solo artisans without staff, the daily tool function must win at the home screen.

---

## Proposed 4-Card Dashboard Layout

A 2×2 card grid, ordered by urgency:

| Position | Card | Purpose |
|----------|------|---------|
| **Top-Left** | **Relances dues** | Overdue invoices — sorted by days overdue, descending |
| **Top-Right** | **Devis en attente** | Quotes with no response > 7 days — sorted by age |
| **Bottom-Left** | **À suivre** | Clients tagged for follow-up today or overdue |
| **Bottom-Right** | **Rendez-vous / Jobs du jour** | Today's scheduled work, if any |

Each card shows: count badge, top 2–3 items as preview, "Voir tout" link.
Tap a card → drills into filtered list view.
Tap an item → opens the record.

**Navigation from Dashboard:**
- "Relances dues" → Relances overdue list → invoice detail
- "Devis en attente" → Pending quotes → quote detail  
- "À suivre" → Follow-up list → client profile
- "Jobs du jour" → Jobs tab, filtered to today

---

## Recommendation

**Dashboard must be home.**

Client Timeline lives as a tertiary view — accessible from the client profile, not from the home screen. The Jobs tab (from Debate 14's resolution) remains accessible from the Dashboard's bottom-right card.

The home screen is not a database. It's a cockpit. And Marc needs to fly before he can land.
